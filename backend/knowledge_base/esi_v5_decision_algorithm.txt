ESI v5 EMERGENCY SEVERITY INDEX — COMPLETE DECISION ALGORITHM
==============================================================
Source: AHRQ Emergency Severity Index Handbook, adapted for SAFE-Triage

OVERVIEW
--------
The Emergency Severity Index (ESI) is a five-level triage algorithm that
stratifies patients into five groups from ESI 1 (most urgent) to ESI 5
(least urgent). The algorithm uses four sequential Decision Points (A-D).
Each patient enters at Decision Point A and exits at the first decision
point that assigns a level.


DECISION POINT A — DOES THIS PATIENT REQUIRE IMMEDIATE LIFE-SAVING INTERVENTION?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If YES → ESI Level 1 (Resuscitation). If NO → proceed to Decision Point B.

Life-saving interventions include:
• Intubation or emergency airway management
• Emergency medications (epinephrine for anaphylaxis, naloxone for overdose)
• Electrical therapy (defibrillation, cardioversion, transcutaneous pacing)
• Procedural sedation for immediate procedures

Objective criteria that trigger ESI 1:
• Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) ≤ 8
• Systolic blood pressure < 70 mmHg
• Heart rate < 30 bpm
• SpO2 < 85% with respiratory distress
• Unresponsive or responds only to pain
• Active seizure (status epilepticus)
• Cardiac or respiratory arrest
• Active massive hemorrhage
• Major trauma with hemodynamic instability
• Penetrating chest wound
• Airway obstruction
• Anaphylaxis with airway compromise
• Tension pneumothorax

NEWS2 integration: Total NEWS2 score ≥ 7 triggers ESI 1.

Stroke FAST protocol: If ≥ 2 FAST components (Face drooping, Arm weakness,
Speech difficulty) or confusion with speech changes → ESI 1.


DECISION POINT B — IS THIS A HIGH-RISK SITUATION? SHOULD THIS PATIENT NOT WAIT?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If YES → ESI Level 2 (Emergent). If NO → proceed to Decision Point C.

High-risk situations:
• Altered mental status: confused, lethargic, disoriented, responds only to voice
• New-onset neurological deficit (stroke symptoms, unilateral weakness)
• Chest pain with cardiac risk factors
• Severe respiratory distress (SpO2 < 90%, RR > 30)
• Hemodynamic instability (SBP 70-90 mmHg, HR > 150 or < 50 while conscious)
• Severe allergic reaction
• Diabetic emergencies (hypoglycemia with altered consciousness, DKA)
• Ectopic pregnancy pattern (reproductive-age female + acute abdominal/back pain
  + abnormal vitals: HR ≥ 100, SBP < 100, RR ≥ 22, SpO2 < 94%)
• Severe pain (≥ 7/10) WITH objective vital sign abnormalities
• Immunocompromised patient with fever

NEWS2 integration: Total NEWS2 score ≥ 5 triggers at minimum ESI 2.

Chest Pain Safety Floor: ALL chest pain presentations are a minimum ESI 2.
This is a safety floor that cannot be overridden.

Context Rules that trigger ESI 2:
• Silent MI in diabetics: Male ≥ 50 or any patient ≥ 65, diabetes,
  GI symptoms (nausea, vomiting, epigastric pain) + HR > 95 or RR > 20
• Atypical ACS in women: Female ≥ 45, GI/cardiac/musculoskeletal
  body system, atypical symptoms (jaw pain, fatigue, nausea)
• Sepsis screening: Immunocompromised/diabetic/elderly/cancer patient +
  sepsis-related body system + fever > 38.5°C + ≥ 2 of (HR > 100,
  RR > 22, altered mental status)


DECISION POINT C — HOW MANY RESOURCES WILL THIS PATIENT NEED?
--------------------------------------------------------------
Resource count determines ESI level:
• 2 or more resources → ESI Level 3 (Urgent)
• 1 resource → ESI Level 4 (Less Urgent)
• 0 resources → ESI Level 5 (Non-Urgent)

What counts as a resource (each item = 1 resource):
• Labs: blood work, urinalysis, cultures
• ECG / EKG
• Imaging: X-ray, CT scan, ultrasound, MRI
• IV fluids or IV medications (NOT oral medications)
• Procedures: sutures, splinting, incision & drainage, catheter
• Specialty consult
• Continuous monitoring (cardiac monitor, continuous pulse oximetry)

What does NOT count as a resource:
• History and physical examination
• Point-of-care tests (fingerstick glucose)
• Oral medications
• Tetanus immunization
• Simple wound care (bandaging)
• Prescription refills
• Discharge instructions


DECISION POINT D — ARE VITAL SIGNS IN THE DANGER ZONE?
-------------------------------------------------------
Applied ONLY to patients triaged ESI 4 or ESI 5. If vital signs are in
the danger zone → upgrade to ESI Level 3.

Danger zone thresholds:
• Heart rate > 100 bpm
• Respiratory rate > 20 breaths/min
• SpO2 < 92%

If any danger zone vital is present AND patient was initially ESI 4 or 5,
the patient is upgraded to ESI 3 for closer monitoring.

SPECIAL POPULATIONS
--------------------

Pediatric vital sign thresholds vary by age:
• Neonate (0-28 days): HR ≤ 205, RR ≤ 60, SpO2 > 92%. Fever ≥ 38°C is
  MANDATORY ESI 2 (sepsis risk).
• Infant 1-3 months: HR ≤ 180, RR ≤ 55. Fever ≥ 38°C → Consider ESI 2.
• Infant 3-12 months: HR ≤ 180, RR ≤ 50. Fever ≥ 39°C → ESI 3.
• Toddler 1-3 years: HR ≤ 160, RR ≤ 40.
• Preschool 3-6 years: HR ≤ 140, RR ≤ 34.
• School 6-12 years: HR ≤ 120, RR ≤ 30.
• Adolescent 12-18: HR ≤ 100, RR ≤ 20.

Geriatric patients:
• Vital signs may be misleadingly normal due to medications (beta-blockers).
• Lower threshold for ESI 2 when comorbidities are present.
• Atypical presentations are more common (painless MI, afebrile sepsis).

Severe pain context:
• Systemic pain (renal colic, sickle cell, cancer) + pain ≥ 7/10 → ESI 2.
• Orthopedic pain (sprain, strain) + pain ≥ 7/10 → ESI 3.
• Pain ≥ 8/10 without clear context → ESI 2.

------------------------------------

• AI confidence < 0.70: Use deterministic ESI, review required.
