NEWS2 NATIONAL EARLY WARNING SCORE 2 — COMPLETE SCORING GUIDE
===============================================================
Source: Royal College of Physicians (RCP), adapted for SAFE-Triage


OVERVIEW
--------
NEWS2 is a standardized early warning score that tracks six physiological
parameters. Each parameter scores 0-3 points. The total score (0-20)
indicates clinical deterioration risk and guides escalation of care.

NEWS2 is used alongside ESI in SAFE-Triage to provide an objective
severity measure. NEWS2 can override ESI assignments upward (never
downward) via safety floor rules.


PARAMETER SCORING TABLE
-------------------------

1. RESPIRATORY RATE (breaths per minute)
   Score 3: ≤ 8
   Score 1: 9-11
   Score 0: 12-20  (normal range)
   Score 2: 21-24
   Score 3: ≥ 25

2. SpO2 OXYGEN SATURATION — Scale 1 (standard patients)
   Score 3: ≤ 91%
   Score 2: 92-93%
   Score 1: 94-95%
   Score 0: ≥ 96%  (normal)

   SpO2 — Scale 2 (COPD or patients on prescribed supplemental O2)
   Score 3: ≤ 83%
   Score 2: 84-85%
   Score 1: 86-87%
   Score 0: 88-92%  (target range for COPD)
   Score 2: 93-94% on room air
   Score 3: ≥ 95% on room air

   Note: Scale 2 is used when the patient has a documented target SpO2
   of 88-92% (e.g., COPD with chronic CO2 retention). If in doubt,
   use Scale 1.

3. SUPPLEMENTAL OXYGEN
   Score 2: Patient is on supplemental oxygen
   Score 0: Patient is on room air

4. SYSTOLIC BLOOD PRESSURE (mmHg)
   Score 3: ≤ 90
   Score 2: 91-100
   Score 1: 101-110
   Score 0: 111-219  (normal range)
   Score 3: ≥ 220

5. HEART RATE (beats per minute)
   Score 3: ≤ 40
   Score 1: 41-50
   Score 0: 51-90  (normal range)
   Score 1: 91-110
   Score 2: 111-130
   Score 3: ≥ 131

6. LEVEL OF CONSCIOUSNESS
   Score 0: Alert (A) — fully awake, eyes open spontaneously
   Score 3: Confusion, Voice, Pain, or Unresponsive (C/V/P/U)

   When using GCS instead of AVPU:
   GCS 15 = Alert (score 0)
   GCS < 15 = Not fully alert (score 3)

7. TEMPERATURE (degrees Celsius)
   Score 3: ≤ 35.0
   Score 1: 35.1-36.0
   Score 0: 36.1-38.0  (normal range)
   Score 1: 38.1-39.0
   Score 2: ≥ 39.1


TOTAL SCORE INTERPRETATION
---------------------------

Score 0:
  Risk level: LOW
  Clinical response: Continue routine monitoring.
  Minimum observation frequency: Every 12 hours.

Score 1-4:
  Risk level: LOW
  Clinical response: Inform registered nurse. Assess if increased monitoring needed.
  Minimum observation frequency: Every 4-6 hours.

Score 3 in any SINGLE parameter:
  Risk level: LOW-MEDIUM
  Clinical response: Urgent review by ward-based doctor. Consider whether
  escalation is needed. The single elevated parameter may indicate an
  organ-specific problem.
  Minimum observation frequency: Every hour.

Score 5-6:
  Risk level: MEDIUM
  Clinical response: Urgent response. Clinician with competence in acute
  illness assessment to review. Consider transfer to higher-dependency
  environment.
  Minimum observation frequency: Every hour.

Score 7 or more:
  Risk level: HIGH
  Clinical response: Emergency response. Immediate assessment by critical
  care team. Transfer to ICU / resuscitation area. Continuous monitoring.
  Minimum observation frequency: Continuous.


NEWS2 INTEGRATION WITH ESI IN SAFE-TRIAGE
------------------------------------------

NEWS2 provides safety floors that can only RAISE the ESI level (never lower it):

• NEWS2 ≥ 7 OR any single parameter scores 3 → Minimum ESI 1
  Rationale: Score ≥ 7 indicates imminent risk of cardiac arrest or organ
  failure. Any single parameter at 3 indicates a critical derangement in
  one organ system.

• NEWS2 ≥ 5 → Minimum ESI 2
  Rationale: Score 5-6 indicates medium risk of deterioration requiring
  urgent medical review.

• NEWS2 ≥ 3 → Minimum ESI 3
  Rationale: Score 3-4 indicates early signs of deterioration requiring
  closer monitoring.

These safety floors are applied AFTER the ESI decision algorithm. If the
ESI algorithm assigns a lower level (e.g., ESI 4) but NEWS2 is ≥ 5, the
patient is upgraded to ESI 2.


CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE OF PARAMETER SCORES
-------------------------------------------

Heart Rate:
  A score of 3 (≤ 40 or ≥ 131) suggests: severe bradycardia requiring
  atropine or pacing; or tachycardia suggesting hypovolemia, sepsis,
  pain, cardiac arrhythmia, or pulmonary embolism.

Respiratory Rate:
  A score of 3 (≤ 8 or ≥ 25) suggests: respiratory depression from opioids,
  neurological injury, or exhaustion; or tachypnea from pneumonia, PE,
  metabolic acidosis, sepsis, or heart failure.

SpO2:
  A score of 3 (≤ 91%) suggests: significant hypoxemia requiring
  supplemental oxygen and investigation. Consider pneumonia, PE, COPD
  exacerbation, pneumothorax, or heart failure.

Systolic BP:
  A score of 3 (≤ 90 or ≥ 220) suggests: hypotension from sepsis,
  hemorrhage, cardiogenic shock, or anaphylaxis; or hypertensive crisis
  requiring urgent treatment.

Temperature:
  A score of 3 (≤ 35.0°C) suggests: hypothermia from environmental
  exposure, sepsis (paradoxical hypothermia is a sign of severe sepsis),
  or metabolic derangement.
  A score of 2 (≥ 39.1°C) suggests: significant fever requiring
  investigation for infection source.

Consciousness:
  A score of 3 (not alert) suggests: neurological emergency (stroke,
  intracranial hemorrhage), metabolic derangement (hypoglycemia, hepatic
  encephalopathy), drug effect, or post-ictal state.


LIMITATIONS OF NEWS2
---------------------
• Does not replace clinical judgment — a patient can be critically ill
  with a low NEWS2 score (e.g., young patient compensating).
• Pain is not included in NEWS2 — severe pain requires separate assessment.
• Does not account for chronic conditions — baseline abnormalities may
  inflate scores (e.g., chronic kidney disease with baseline low BP).
• Pediatric patients require age-adjusted vital sign thresholds (NEWS2
  is validated for adults only).
• Pregnancy alters normal vital sign ranges (lower BP, higher HR).
