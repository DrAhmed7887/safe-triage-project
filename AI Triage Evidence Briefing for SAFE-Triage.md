# **Clinical AI Literature Review and Product Evidence Brief: SAFE-Triage System**

## **Executive Summary**

The integration of artificial intelligence and large language models (LLMs) into emergency department (ED) triage presents a profound opportunity to enhance clinical decision-making, optimize scarce resource allocation, and mitigate the risks of occult patient deterioration. This comprehensive evidence synthesis evaluates the clinical, scientific, and technical foundations of SAFE-Triage, an AI-assisted triage decision support system designed to operate in high-acuity, overcrowded clinical environments. The analysis rigorously examines the validity of combining the National Early Warning Score 2 (NEWS2) with the Emergency Severity Index (ESI) to form a hybrid, computationally driven triage model.

Current medical literature demonstrates that while NEWS2 is a robust, highly validated physiological track-and-trigger system with strong predictive value for early mortality and intensive care unit (ICU) admission, it fundamentally lacks the diagnostic breadth and resource-estimation logic necessary to function as a standalone emergency triage tool. Conversely, ESI provides a universally recognized framework for sorting patients based on clinical acuity and anticipated hospital resource utilization. However, ESI remains highly susceptible to subjective interpretation, inter-rater variability, cognitive overload, and documented ethnoracial biases. A hybrid AI model effectively leverages the strengths of both systems: the objective, deterministic physiological safety net of NEWS2 and the comprehensive, narrative-driven clinical complaint logic of ESI.

Furthermore, this report establishes that the evaluation of generative AI for emergency triage necessitates highly specialized, structured datasets. Raw, unstructured triage notes, such as those found in the foundational MIMIC-IV-ED dataset, are characterized by extreme abbreviation, dangerous data leakage, and stylistic inconsistencies that severely degrade narrative model performance and evaluation. The MIMIC-IV-Ext Triage Instruction Corpus (MIETIC) resolves these deficiencies by providing expert-validated, ESI-aligned, instruction-tuned data, establishing it as the superior and mandatory benchmark for training, validating, and auditing SAFE-Triage. Finally, a multiphasic, silent-mode clinical validation plan is recommended to safely translate this technology into local ED workflows, particularly in resource-constrained or organizationally complex settings like Egyptian emergency departments. This validation strategy emphasizes the absolute minimization of critical under-triage as the paramount metric for patient safety and algorithmic deployment.

## **A. Clinical Rationale for NEWS2**

The National Early Warning Score 2 (NEWS2) is fundamentally designed as a physiological track-and-trigger mechanism to identify acute clinical deterioration. Developed and endorsed by the Royal College of Physicians, it assigns weighted numerical scores to six distinct physiological parameters: respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, systolic blood pressure, pulse rate, level of consciousness (or new-onset confusion), and temperature.12 The clinical rationale for incorporating NEWS2 into a triage system is firmly rooted in its exceptional ability to quantify the immediate physiological stability of a patient. It serves as an objective, mathematically deterministic counterbalance to the highly subjective elements of narrative patient assessment.

Extensive literature analysis indicates that NEWS2 exhibits robust predictive validity for short-term, high-acuity outcomes across diverse clinical environments. In emergency and prehospital settings, NEWS2 provides highly significant statistical discrimination for predicting the need for immediate critical care interventions. For instance, a meta-analysis of ED patients demonstrates that an aggregate NEWS2 score provides adequate prediction for intensive care unit (ICU) admission (Area Under the Curve \[AUC\] 0.716, Confidence Interval \[CI\] 0.690–0.742) and the necessity of critical care delivery within the ED itself (AUC 0.756, CI 0.732–0.780).2 Patients registering a NEWS2 score greater than 4 exhibit a substantially elevated odds ratio (OR 2.339, p\<0.001) for imminent ICU admission.2 During the peak of the COVID-19 pandemic, NEWS2 was further validated as an acceptable predictor of short-term 2-day mortality (AUC 0.77), significantly outperforming any single isolated physiological parameter such as oxygen saturation (AUC 0.65) or respiratory rate alone (AUC 0.64).6

A critical distinction must be made regarding the exact nature of the evidence: NEWS2 is a predictor of patient deterioration and immediate physiological threat, not a comprehensive diagnostic emergency triage tool. It excels at identifying the "sick versus not sick" dichotomy at the purely physiological level. Older adults, particularly those living in care homes with advanced frailty and multimorbidity, often present to the ED with highly atypical symptoms that mask the severe nature of their underlying illness.14 In these vulnerable cohorts, standard complaint-based triage systems frequently fail to recognize the severity of the presentation. Yet, in prospective observational trials of frail patients (aged ![][image2] 75 years), NEWS2 has demonstrated a superior capacity to predict 30-day mortality (AUC 0.70) and high dependency unit admissions (AUC 0.72) compared to standard 3-level complaint-based triage assessments (AUC 0.62 and 0.80 respectively, varying by specific outcome).3 By quantifying the physiological deviation from homeostasis, NEWS2 forces a clinical response escalation—such as an urgent review by a ward-based doctor for scores of 5 or 6, or an immediate emergency assessment by a critical care team for scores of 7 and above.13

However, it must be explicitly stated that NEWS2 is an adjunct score and should not under any circumstances be presented as a standalone triage replacement. It evaluates the physiological end-result of a disease state, completely agnostic to the underlying pathology, the anticipated hospital resources required, or the patient's chief complaint. A patient may have a perfectly normal NEWS2 score of zero but still require time-sensitive, life-saving interventions for a condition that has not yet caused systemic hemodynamic collapse.16

## **B. Clinical Rationale for ESI**

The Emergency Severity Index (ESI) is the predominant, most extensively validated triage system utilized in modern global emergency medicine. It conceptually differs from physiological early warning scores by integrating clinical acuity with expected diagnostic and therapeutic resource utilization.17 ESI is a summative, algorithmic, five-level categorization system. Levels 1 and 2 prioritize patients requiring immediate, life-saving interventions or those in high-risk situations—such as a threatened airway, severe poly-trauma, suspected acute ischemic stroke, or acute coronary syndrome.16 For patients deemed physiologically stable and not in immediate extremis (Levels 3, 4, and 5), ESI uniquely shifts its decision logic away from vital signs and toward estimating the absolute number of distinct resources (e.g., intravenous fluids, complex cross-sectional imaging, specialty consultations, minor procedures) required to safely disposition the patient.17

The scientific and operational defensibility of ESI lies in its well-documented inter-rater reliability and its direct mathematical correlation with ED operational metrics and throughput efficiency. Systematic reviews covering decades of implementation demonstrate that ESI possesses strong reliability, with inter-rater agreement (weighted kappa statistics) frequently ranging from 0.70 to 0.89 across varied adult populations.1 This represents a massive operational improvement over legacy, non-standardized three-tier triage systems.1 ESI assignment by trained triage nurses is a proven, highly accurate predictor of hospital admission rates, the location of that admission (floor versus step-down), and overall ED length of stay.20 For example, large-scale validity cohorts reveal a clear, linear stratification of hospitalization rates corresponding directly to ESI levels: 83% admission for Level 1, 67% for Level 2, 42% for Level 3, dropping precipitously to 8% for Level 4 and 4% for Level 5\.22 Similarly, 60-day all-cause mortality rates scale perfectly with ESI categorization (25% for Level 1 down to 0% for Level 5).22

Explicitly stated, ESI provides an acuity and expected resource use logic that is simply not captured by NEWS2 alone. A patient presenting with an isolated, stable long-bone fracture or a deep laceration will have perfectly normal vital signs, generating a NEWS2 score of 0\. However, this patient requires multiple ED resources: radiographs, orthopedic consultation, intravenous analgesia, and procedural sedation. ESI correctly categorizes this patient as a Level 3, reserving definitive care areas and signaling to the ED flow manager that this patient will consume significant time and space, preventing the patient from waiting indefinitely as a Level 5 non-urgent case.17 ESI constitutes the structural and operational framework of the ED queue; it translates human clinical intuition, chief complaints, and anticipated diagnostic pathways into a universal, actionable language of patient sorting.17

## **C. Why Combine NEWS2 \+ ESI**

The clinical and scientific rationale for a hybrid triage model utilizing both NEWS2 and ESI lies in the perfectly complementary nature of their respective operational blind spots. When utilized in isolation, each system exhibits distinct vulnerabilities that can lead to catastrophic medical errors, specifically critical under-triage. By computationally fusing the two via an AI decision-support architecture, the system establishes a reciprocal, multi-modal safety net.

ESI, while highly comprehensive in its resource allocation, is inherently subjective. It relies profoundly on the individual triage nurse's clinical gestalt, years of experience, current cognitive load, and interpretation of often-vague patient narratives.4 This subjectivity inevitably leads to systemic misclassification. Large-scale retrospective analyses reveal alarming error rates in raw ESI application, with generalized under-triage rates reported at 3.3% and over-triage rates approaching 28.9% in some multi-center cohorts.5 Most concerningly, the sensitivity of human-applied ESI for identifying critically ill patients (those genuinely requiring Level 1 or 2 categorization) has been recorded as low as 65.9% in overloaded community settings.5 ESI routinely under-triages patients presenting with complex, non-specific complaints (e.g., generalized weakness, dizziness), vague neurological symptoms, or the atypical infectious presentations common in geriatric and immunocompromised populations.19

In stark contrast, NEWS2 is purely objective and mathematically deterministic, but entirely lacks clinical context. If a department relies solely on NEWS2, it will dramatically and dangerously under-triage specific, highly lethal clinical phenotypes. A quintessential example is a patient experiencing an acute ischemic stroke, stable ectopic pregnancy, or unstable angina. These patients frequently present to the intake desk with completely stable vital signs—a normal respiratory rate, pristine oxygen saturation, and regular pulse—yielding a benign NEWS2 score of 0 or 1\. However, their condition is intensely time-sensitive and life-threatening, requiring immediate ESI Level 2 categorization for time-critical interventions such as thrombolytics or emergency cardiac catheterization.16 Relying exclusively on a physiological early warning score would dangerously delay care for these critical, yet hemodynamically compensated, patients.29

By combining the two methodologies, the hybrid model achieves a superior, synergistic synthesis of patient data. The generative AI and ESI algorithmic logic drive the contextual categorization of the chief complaint, the extraction of high-risk scenarios (such as subtle descriptions of chest pain or sudden-onset weakness), and the prediction of ED diagnostic resource consumption. Simultaneously, the continuous calculation of NEWS2 acts as an unbreakable physiological floor. If an exhausted triage clinician incorrectly categorizes an elderly patient complaining of "tiredness" as an ESI Level 4, an AI system running the hybrid model will instantly compute an elevated NEWS2 score driven by subtle, visually imperceptible hypoxia and tachypnea, immediately flagging the patient for a forced ESI Level 2 upgrade.3 This integration drastically decreases the mathematical probability of under-triaging high-acuity patients, ensuring that human cognitive bias, institutional overcrowding, or simple fatigue does not override objective, measurable signs of systemic decompensation.30

## **D. Risks and Limitations**

Despite the robust theoretical framework of a hybrid triage model, significant clinical limitations, algorithmic risks, and operational vulnerabilities persist. These must be aggressively managed, clearly documented, and actively mitigated through system design and user education.

The most prominent limitation of NEWS2 is its nature as a static, point-in-time assessment. Emergency triage is a fluid, dynamic environment, and a single set of vital signs captured at the intake desk provides only a temporary snapshot of a patient's physiological trajectory. Research indicates that NEWS2 exhibits relatively poor performance in predicting 30-day mortality or prolonged in-hospital outcomes based exclusively on a single triage reading.31 To maximize its predictive accuracy, physiological parameters must be monitored continuously; a patient presenting with a benign NEWS2 score at minute zero may rapidly decompensate into florid septic shock by minute forty-five. Furthermore, the evidence base for NEWS2 is poorly calibrated for specific, highly prevalent subpopulations. It generates a high rate of false positives (over-triggering alerts) in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) who are physiologically adapted to lower baseline oxygen saturations and baseline hypercapnia.32 Conversely, it may yield highly dangerous false negatives in end-stage renal disease (hemodialysis) patients, where alternative scoring systems like qSOFA often provide higher specificity and sensitivity for early sepsis prediction.33 The clinical value of NEWS2 diminishes when applied rigidly, without human clinical judgment, to patients living with baseline frailty or chronic, long-term physiological derangements.14

Conversely, the risks associated with ESI center on human subjectivity, systemic bias, and the immense cognitive burden of rapid resource estimation. Because the crucial transition from ESI Level 3 to Level 2 relies heavily on the subjective determination of a "high-risk situation" or "severe pain/distress," assignment practices vary drastically across institutions, regions, and even between individual nurses on the same shift.4 Deep, systemic biases have been extensively documented; sophisticated causal inference models analyzing millions of encounters indicate that ethnoracial identity significantly and independently influences triage outcomes. Black and Hispanic patients frequently experience unwarranted, statistically significant under-triage compared to White patients presenting with identical clinical symptoms and vital signs.4

Furthermore, categorizing patients into ESI Levels 3 through 5 demands an accurate mental calculation of anticipated hospital resources. Novice clinicians, or those operating in overcrowded and chaotic environments—such as the densely populated, resource-strained EDs prevalent in Egyptian healthcare settings where formal triage systems are often historically lacking 36—often wildly miscalculate these needs. This results in massive over-triage (assigning Level 2 or 3 to minor complaints) out of an abundance of caution, which paralyzes ED flow, consumes critical care beds, and delays care for truly sick patients.5 AI systems must be calibrated to recognize and interrupt these limitations. If the LLM blindly trusts historical human ESI labels from flawed datasets without adjusting for documented ethnoracial, age-related, or resource-driven mistriage, it will simply automate, scale, and codify human bias at algorithmic speeds.

## **E. How MIETIC Should Be Used**

The transition from traditional, deterministic triage algorithms to modern, LLM-driven narrative reasoning requires exceptionally high-quality, specialized data. In the domain of medical AI, researchers frequently attempt to train and evaluate models on the raw, open-source MIMIC-IV-ED dataset. However, it must be explicitly explained that raw MIMIC-IV-ED triage complaint strings are severely abbreviated, structurally chaotic, riddled with typographical errors, and entirely inadequate for evaluating complex narrative reasoning models.9 In raw electronic health record (EHR) data, the "chief complaint" is often a fragmented, acronym-heavy string of text entered under immense time pressure by a triage nurse. It is stripped of the conversational context, non-verbal cues, and historical continuity necessary for an LLM to derive a holistic clinical picture. Furthermore, raw clinical notes frequently suffer from dangerous data leakage, wherein post-triage interventions, discharge summaries, or subsequent definitive diagnostic findings (like X-ray results) are inadvertently included in the text field, allowing an AI model to artificially "cheat" by accessing future data to make an ostensibly predictive triage categorization.7

To resolve these critical structural and methodological issues, the MIMIC-IV-Ext Triage Instruction Corpus (MIETIC) was explicitly constructed. MIETIC is a highly structured, instruction-based dataset specifically designed to support LLM development and benchmarking for emergency triage tasks.7 It contains over 9,600 meticulously filtered cases mapped directly to standardized ESI criteria. MIETIC must be utilized as the foundational data architecture for the SAFE-Triage system for three distinct, scientifically defensible reasons:

1. **Instruction-Tuning Architecture**: Unlike disjointed raw EHR logs, MIETIC formats data into strict, LLM-friendly instruction-input-output pairs. This specific format allows the LLM to undergo supervised fine-tuning and domain adaptation, learning context-driven decision-making by seamlessly synthesizing demographics, vital signs, and narrative patient descriptions to output a clinically justified ESI level.7  
2. **Eradication of Data Leakage**: MIETIC's construction utilized rigorous, multi-step SQL-based filtering to identify and eliminate all post-triage information. The model is computationally forced to predict acuity using only the strictly limited information genuinely available to a triage nurse at the precise moment of patient presentation, ensuring real-world validity.7  
3. **Error Analysis and Gold-Standard Benchmarking**: The dataset includes MIETIC-validate-samples.csv, a highly specialized subset of 50 patient encounters that have undergone rigorous manual validation and cross-examination by three independent human medical experts.7 In cases of disagreement between the first two experts, the third expert provided the definitive, adjudicated classification.

SAFE-Triage should utilize the broader MIETIC corpus for domain adaptation, prompt optimization, and fine-tuning the underlying LLM.8 Subsequently, the MIETIC-validate-samples.csv must be entirely isolated and used exclusively as a hold-out test set for validation and error analysis. When SAFE-Triage makes an error or deviates on this 50-sample set, clinical developers can directly compare the LLM's generated narrative reasoning against the documented, step-by-step rationale of the three human experts. This allows for highly targeted prompt engineering, algorithmic calibration, and systemic safety updates. Furthermore, all public dashboard claims regarding the AI's accuracy, sensitivity, and reasoning quality must be derived strictly from performance against this specific, expert-adjudicated subset to ensure clinical defensibility and regulatory compliance.

## **G. Recommended Validation Plan for SAFE-Triage**

The introduction of an artificial intelligence triage system into live clinical practice poses profound patient safety and medicolegal risks if deployed prematurely or evaluated poorly.47 Therefore, an exhaustive, multi-dimensional, three-stage validation plan is mandated to transition SAFE-Triage from theoretical capability to safe, localized deployment. This plan adheres to the highest standards of clinical implementation science.

### **Stage 1: Deterministic Rules \+ NEWS2 \+ Complaint-Based Logic**

Prior to the involvement of complex, non-deterministic generative language models, the system must demonstrate absolute, flawless execution of established clinical deterministic rules. This foundational stage requires testing the system's core algorithmic logic against a synthetic, stress-tested dataset of edge-case clinical presentations. The system must prove, with 100% reliability, that it can accurately compute the NEWS2 score from a string of raw vital signs. Furthermore, it must demonstrate the ability to automatically trigger a forced ESI Level 1 or 2 upgrade when specific, life-threatening red-flag parameters are detected (e.g., ![][image3], unresponsiveness to voice/pain) or when specific pathognomonic chief complaints are entered (e.g., "active crushing chest pain," "sudden unilateral facial droop").13 This verifies the underlying, unbreakable safety-net architecture before introducing the variability of LLM reasoning.

### **Stage 2: MIETIC Validation Using Final Decision**

Once deterministic rules are validated, the LLM-driven narrative reasoning component must be rigorously evaluated using the MIETIC-validate-samples.csv. This 50-sample, expert-adjudicated dataset serves as the absolute, gold-standard ground truth.7 The model's predictions must be scored across a specific matrix of rigorous clinical metrics:

* **Exact ESI Agreement:** The percentage of times the AI's predicted ESI level perfectly matches the 5-level expert consensus. While important, this is not the most critical safety metric.  
* **Within-One-Level Agreement:** The percentage of times the AI is ![][image4] level from the expert consensus, indicating general alignment with the clinical trajectory.  
* **Over-Triage Rate:** The frequency with which the AI assigns a higher acuity (lower numerical ESI) than the ground truth, which impacts hospital resource availability.5  
* **Under-Triage Rate:** The frequency with which the AI assigns a lower acuity (higher numerical ESI) than the ground truth, exposing the patient to potential harm through delayed care.5  
* **Critical Under-Triage:** Strictly defined as assigning a patient two or more triage categories lower than their true gold-standard acuity (e.g., triaging a true Level 2 critical patient as a Level 4 non-urgent).52 This must be minimized to near zero.  
* **Sensitivity for ESI-1/2:** The model's statistical ability to accurately capture and flag the sickest, most time-critical patients.  
* **Specificity for ESI-4/5:** The model's statistical ability to safely and confidently rule out severe illness in low-acuity patients, allowing them to be routed to fast-track areas.  
* **Calibration:** A statistical measure evaluating how well the AI's predicted probability of an ESI level matches the true empirical frequency of that level in the real world. Poor calibration leads to alert fatigue.  
* **Narrative Reasoning Quality:** A qualitative assessment by human clinicians of the LLM's generated textual explanation. The reasoning must cite relevant ESI handbook guidelines and physiological data, remaining entirely free of medical hallucinations or fabricated clinical signs.7

### **Stage 3: Prospective Local Silent-Mode Validation in Egyptian ED Workflow**

The final and most crucial phase is a prospective, "silent mode" validation conducted locally. This is particularly critical if deployment is intended for Egyptian emergency departments or similar environments. Research explicitly indicates that Egyptian healthcare systems frequently face unique workflow challenges, massive overcrowding, a historical lack of formalized national triage structures, and highly restricted capabilities for nursing scope of practice compared to Western models.36

In silent mode, the SAFE-Triage software is integrated into the hospital's electronic health record (EHR) but runs entirely in the background of the live clinical environment. It analyzes real-time incoming patient data and generates triage scores that are completely hidden from the treating clinicians.47 This methodology provides early safety signals and identifies algorithmic deficiencies without risking patient harm, introducing automation bias, or increasing the medicolegal risk to the facility.48 Data must be collected over a minimum 3 to 6-month period.49 The primary endpoints of this stage are **clinician agreement** (comparing the AI's hidden score to the nurse's actual assigned score) and an extensive, multi-disciplinary error analysis of the discordance between human action and AI recommendation, ensuring the tool is safe for the specific local population.

### **The Primacy of Minimizing Critical Under-Triage**

Across all stages of validation, performance reporting must overwhelmingly emphasize the absolute minimization of critical under-triage over the maximization of simple exact-match accuracy. In the operational mathematics and ethics of emergency medicine, the cost functions of triage errors are wildly asymmetrical. Over-triage (false positives) results in temporary operational inefficiencies, longer waits for low-acuity patients, and higher immediate resource utilization.5 While undesirable, it is rarely lethal.

Conversely, under-triage (false negatives) results in delayed definitive care, unrecognized physiological decompensation, irreversible tissue ischemia, and preventable patient death.45 Critical under-triage—missing a severe trauma, an actively occluding myocardial infarction, or covert septic shock—is a catastrophic system failure.52 Therefore, an AI system that achieves 80% exact-match accuracy but has a mathematically proven 0% critical under-triage rate is vastly superior, significantly safer, and more clinically defensible than a system with 92% exact-match accuracy that occasionally triages stroke patients to the waiting room. The absolute threshold for algorithmic deployment must be the elimination of critical under-triage.44

## **Do Not Claim: Prohibited Overstatements**

To maintain strict clinical integrity, regulatory compliance, and medical conservatism, SAFE-Triage marketing, public documentation, and internal training materials must strictly avoid the following types of overstatements and dangerous generalizations:

* **Do not claim to diagnose medical conditions:**  
  * *Example to avoid:* "SAFE-Triage accurately diagnoses your emergency condition and prescribes the right priority."  
  * *Correction:* Triage is an operational sorting mechanism based on symptom presentation and physiological threat, not a definitive diagnostic procedure. The system estimates acuity and anticipated resource need.  
* **Do not claim to replace clinical judgment or nursing staff:**  
  * *Example to avoid:* "Our AI replaces the need for triage nurses, automating the entire ED intake process."  
  * *Correction:* AI is strictly a decision-support adjunct. The system explicitly relies on a hybrid framework where human clinical judgment oversees, verifies, and ultimately authorizes the algorithmic output.  
* **Do not present NEWS2 as a standalone triage solution:**  
  * *Example to avoid:* "SAFE-Triage uses the NEWS2 algorithm to assign your ED wait time and bed placement."  
  * *Correction:* NEWS2 detects physiological deterioration; it possesses no mechanism for predicting hospital resource consumption, specialized pathways (like stroke protocols), or workflow queues. It is the physiological safety net, while ESI acts as the queue manager.  
* **Do not claim 100% safety or the total elimination of all medical errors:**  
  * *Example to avoid:* "SAFE-Triage completely eliminates all triage errors and patient risk in the ED."  
  * *Correction:* Real-world medical data is inherently noisy, chaotic, and frequently incomplete. The system *mitigates*, *standardizes*, and *reduces* variance and systemic bias, but zero-error environments in high-acuity emergency medicine do not exist.  
* **Do not misrepresent the nature of the training data:**  
  * *Example to avoid:* "Our LLM was trained on millions of perfect, real-world MIMIC-IV triage notes, making it infinitely wise."  
  * *Correction:* Raw MIMIC-IV-ED notes are highly flawed, abbreviated, and suffer from data leakage. Claims must transparently specify the use of expertly curated, structured, instruction-tuned subsets like MIETIC to maintain scientific credibility.

SAFE-Triage should currently describe itself on the dashboard as an investigational, AI-assisted clinical decision support system designed to augment emergency department triage by safely synthesizing complex patient data. It must clearly frame its unique utility around the synergistic combination of continuous physiological safety netting (via NEWS2) and standardized, predictive resource estimation (via ESI), emphasizing its role in mitigating cognitive overload, reducing subjective human bias, and computationally preventing dangerous under-triage. The platform should explicitly and prominently state that it does not replace the critical, in-person clinical judgment, visual assessment, and empathy of trained emergency personnel, but rather serves as a rigorously designed, highly validated computational safeguard optimized for high-volume, dynamic, and unpredictable clinical environments.
